                        <div>                             <h1 style="text-align: center;"><strong>Humanities, Law, and an Inflection Point for Our Constitution</strong></h1>
<h2><strong>Overview</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The United States Constitution remains one of the most influential - and contested - frameworks shaping American democracy. At a time of political polarization and renewed scrutiny of institutions, questions about constitutional interpretation, stability, and authority have taken on new urgency. This symposium approaches the Constitution as more than a legal document, examining it as a historical, linguistic, and cultural text shaped through interpretation over time.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Drawing on the idea of an </span><strong>inflection point</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> - a moment when direction shifts while continuity remains - this symposium brings together scholars in law and the humanities to reflect on how enduring constitutional principles are being tested and reinterpreted today. By reconnecting legal analysis with humanistic inquiry, the symposium highlights how history, literature, philosophy, and rhetoric deepen our understanding of constitutional meaning and democratic life.</span></p>
<p>&#160;</p>
                        </div></div>                                                                                             
            <div class="grid column2  blue  boxes  js-list">
    
                  
                                                        <div>
                                                                        <h3><strong>Why the Humanities Matter</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Understanding the Constitution requires more than legal analysis. The humanities&#8212;history, philosophy, literature, religion, and political thought&#8212;offer the context and interpretive tools needed to examine its meaning and impact.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The nation's founders did not separate law from the humanities. That shared foundation has since fragmented. This symposium returns to those foundations, bringing humanistic and legal perspectives back into conversation.</span></p>
                                                                                                                    </div><div>
                                                                <h3><strong>A Broader Moment: America250</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This symposium kicks off Carnegie Mellon University&#8217;s America250 programming, commemorating the 250th anniversary of American independence.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Held on Constitution Day, the event anchors a year-long set of programs that reflect on the nation&#8217;s founding documents while engaging the questions that will shape its future.</span></p>
                                </div>
            </div>

<p style="margin: 0; font-size: 1.1rem; line-height: 1.75;">As the nation marks its Semiquincentennial, the keynote will revisit the relationship between the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution, asking what it means to speak of &#8220;We the People&#8221; today.                                                                         <p style="text-align: center; color: #c41230;"><strong>A Special Presentation and Reception at the Posner Center for Special Collections | 4:45 p.m</strong></p>
<p>The US Constitution as Object &#8211; Material and Book History</p>
<p>Participants will have the rare opportunity to view the Posner Center's copy of the United States Bill of Rights&#8212;one of only five known surviving copies of the first official printing issued in 1792. Hosted in the Posner Center for Special Collections, this session will explore the Bill of Rights not only as a foundational constitutional text, but also as a remarkably fragile material artifact of American history.</p>
                                                                                                                    </div><div>
                                                                <p style="text-align: center; color: #c41230;"><strong>A Special Closing Performance | 6:00 p.m. | Mellon Learning Hall, POS A35, Posner Hall</strong></p>
<p>Explores the Constitution through theatre, poetry, and music as both a historical text and a living source of artistic inspiration, reflecting on its enduring legacy, evolving interpretations, and continuing role in American public life.</p>
