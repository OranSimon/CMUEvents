                <div id="paragraph-278" class="paragraph paragraph--type--p-wysiwyg paragraph--view-mode--default Section Section--centered-left">
      <div>
        
            <h2>About the Carnegie-Rochester-NYU Conference on Public Policy</h2><p>The <strong>Carnegie-Rochester-NYU Conference on Public Policy (CRNYU)</strong> seeks to stimulate policy relevance and empirical research in economic science, to encourage interchange of scientific ideas among analysts with different approaches, and to generate greater understanding by academic economists of practitioners' environments.</p><p>Each conference is organized around a particular theme or topic with papers prepared by leading scholars with expertise in the area. Participants are united by their interest in the issues discussed and by their belief that analysis, evidence, and informed discussion have lasting effects on the public and its institutions. The conference receives financial support from the <a href="https://www.nsf.gov/" target="_blank">National Science Foundation</a> and from its host institutions.</p>
      
      </div>
    </div>
  
                <div id="paragraph-279" class="paragraph paragraph--type--p-wysiwyg paragraph--view-mode--default Section Section--centered-left">
      <div>
        
            <h2>Conference History</h2><p>The<strong> </strong>CRNYU was initiated in the early 1970s through the efforts of the Bradley Policy Research Center at the <a href="https://simon.rochester.edu/" target="_blank">William E. Simon School of Business Administration</a> at the University of Rochester and the Center for the Study of Public Policy at Carnegie Mellon University.</p><p>Under the leadership of the late Allan Meltzer (Carnegie Mellon University) and the late Karl Brunner (University of Rochester), the conference developed into a semi-annual event.</p><p>Subsequently, <a href="https://www.stern.nyu.edu/" target="_blank">New York University's Stern School of Business</a> joined Carnegie Mellon’s Tepper School of Business and the University of Rochester’s Simon School of Business as a host institution.</p><p>Today, the conference takes place annually in the spring of each year and rotates between each host institution.</p>
      
      </div>
    </div>
  
                <div id="paragraph-365" class="paragraph paragraph--type--p-wysiwyg paragraph--view-mode--default Section Section--centered-left">
      <div>
        
            <h2>Notable Publications</h2><p><br>Robert Lucas’s seminal paper on the econometric evaluation of policy appeared in the first issue of the conference.</p><p><br><a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167223176800036" target="_blank">R.E. Lucas (1976). ''Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique”. Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy, 1:19-46.</a></p><p><br>John Taylor’s paper in 1993 introduced the Taylor Rule and transformed discussion and analysis of monetary policy.</p><p><br><a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/016722319390009L" target="_blank">John Taylor (1993). ''Discretion versus policy rules in practice’’. Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy, 39:195-214.</a></p>
      
      </div>
