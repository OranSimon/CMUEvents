Talk Title: Signal is More Than the Signal Protocol: A Tour of
Vulnerabilities the Proofs Missed

The Signal Protocol is among the most heavily analyzed cryptographic
protocols ever deployed: pen-and-paper proofs, symbolic models, and
machine-checked computational proofs all attest to its security. Those
results are real. Nevertheless, over the years we have found — and
fixed — vulnerabilities in Signal that degraded the security of real
conversations.

This talk surveys a few of them, ranging from the core protocol
implementation to product features layered well above it. For each, I
answer the obvious question: what did the proofs actually cover, and
why did the deployed system fall outside that coverage? These were not
errors in the analysis. They were gaps between the mathematical
abstractions and the engineering ones.

To put those cases in context, I show a bird's-eye view of Signal's
codebase: how small a fraction is the protocol proper, and how much of
the rest determines the security users actually experience. I close
with how I think the research community and industry can narrow the
gap, and how advances in formal verification are letting us analyze
the code that ships.

—

Rolfe Schmidt is a Research Engineer at Signal Messenger, where he
works to bridge academic research with Signal's engineering and
security needs. He co-designed the PQXDH key agreement protocol and
the SPQR post-quantum ratchet, and has worked on Signal's ORAM-backed
contact discovery, Secure Value Recovery, and anonymous credential
systems. He collaborates widely on formal analysis of deployed
protocols.
